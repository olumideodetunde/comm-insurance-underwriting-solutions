03

The lifecycle every milestone repeats

Seven stages, identical for M1 through M7. Stages 5 and 6 are where the Hastings Direct gap gets closed, every single time.

Actuarial baseline
Fit the classical Loss Data Analytics technique in a Databricks notebook against the silver layer. Test distributional assumptions explicitly — e.g. an overdispersion test to choose Poisson vs. negative binomial, or AIC/QQ plots to choose gamma vs. lognormal severity.
GLM/GBM challenger
Same target and features, gradient boosting or a regularised GLM. Match the GBM objective to the actuarial distribution family where possible — Poisson, gamma, Tweedie — the alignment itself is the differentiator between an actuarially-literate GBM and a naive one.
MLflow tracking
Log distribution family, link function, and hyperparameters as params. Log Poisson/gamma deviance, normalised Gini, calibration-by-decile, and double-lift vs. baseline as metrics — not just RMSE. Log lift charts, residual diagnostics, and SHAP or coefficient tables as artifacts.
Registration
Register champion and challenger under a fixed catalog.schema.model_name convention in Unity Catalog, using alias-based promotion (@champion / @challenger) rather than legacy stage names.
Deploy as an endpoint closes Hastings Direct
Deploy the champion as a Databricks Model Serving CPU real-time endpoint. Free Edition's serving reliability for custom models is unconfirmed — test this in M1, and fall back to a scheduled batch-scoring job if it fails. Naming the fallback honestly is itself a deployment story worth telling.
Monitor closes Hastings Direct
A scheduled job scores a fresh slice weekly, computes feature and prediction drift (PSI or mean/std) against the training-time baseline logged in MLflow, and logs the result as a new tagged run — a visible time series of model health.
Handwritten debrief
Answer by hand, for interview use: why this distributional choice, and what diagnostic backs it; where the GBM beat the GLM and where the GLM's interpretability mattered more; what broke in deployment/monitoring on Free Edition and what would differ on paid infrastructure; what the next refinement would be with two more weeks.

05

Risks worth naming

Stated plainly rather than glossed over — this is a reference plan to push back on, not a locked spec.

Free Edition serving reliability is unconfirmed
Community reports show "container creation failed" on custom MLflow endpoints on Free Edition. Test in M1, not the capstone, and treat the batch-scoring fallback as real planned work.
Dataset schema is unknown
The Spanish motor dataset hasn't been inspected in this session. Silver-layer goals stay general — no invented column names — until Olu audits the real schema.
Existing repo structure is unknown
Whether the underwriting-assessor repo will cleanly take a milestone structure (issues, branches, tags) can't be assessed sight unseen. A short structural audit before M0 kickoff.
Time budget is unknown
Eight milestones × a seven-stage lifecycle is substantial. M0, M1, M2, M4, M6 are the critical path — the two feedback gaps plus the Pillar-3 gap, covered even if nothing else gets built.
Free Edition job ceilings
Max 5 concurrent job tasks and one active pipeline per pipeline type. Stagger each milestone's weekly monitoring job to a different time slot.
2X-Small SQL warehouse may be slow for simulation
Monte Carlo aggregate-loss simulation (M5/M6) is compute-heavy. Run simulation in a notebook via pandas/numpy rather than the SQL warehouse, or subsample.
Credibility theory has no clean GBM analogue
Plain GBM doesn't do shrinkage natively, unlike Bühlmann–Straub. Use a hierarchical GLMM as the fairer "modern" comparator in M4, and make articulating that distinction part of the debrief itself.
