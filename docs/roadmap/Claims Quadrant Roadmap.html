03

The lifecycle every milestone repeats

Identical to the underwriting roadmap's template — repeated here so this document stands alone. Stages 5 and 6 are where the Hastings Direct gap gets closed, every time.

Statistical baseline
Fit the classical technique for this milestone in a Databricks notebook against the silver layer. Test assumptions explicitly — e.g. proportional-hazards checks for a Cox model, or AIC/QQ diagnostics for a severity GLM.
GLM/GBM challenger
Same target and features, gradient boosting or a regularised GLM. Where the classical method is a survival model, compare against a gradient-boosted survival model or a calibrated GBM classifier on duration/event buckets — not a naive regression that ignores censoring.
MLflow tracking
Log distribution/model family and hyperparameters as params. Log concordance index (survival models), precision-recall/PR-AUC under class imbalance (fraud, litigation), deviance or calibration-by-decile (cost models) as metrics — never accuracy alone on an imbalanced target. Log calibration plots, Kaplan–Meier curves, and confusion matrices as artifacts.
Registration
Register champion and challenger under the same catalog.schema.model_name convention established in the underwriting roadmap's M0, with alias-based promotion (@champion / @challenger).
Deploy as an endpoint closes Hastings Direct
Deploy the champion as a Databricks Model Serving CPU real-time endpoint, same Free Edition caveat and batch-scoring fallback as the underwriting roadmap — already tested there in M1, so this repeats a known pattern rather than re-discovering the risk.
Monitor closes Hastings Direct
A scheduled job scores a fresh slice weekly and tracks drift against the training-time baseline. For imbalanced targets (fraud, litigation) also track the positive-rate over time — a silent shift there is a common real-world failure mode worth naming in interviews.
Handwritten debrief
Answer by hand: why this technique over the alternative (e.g. why competing-risks over a plain binary litigation flag) and what diagnostic backs it; where the GBM beat the classical baseline and where interpretability mattered more to a claims stakeholder; what the class-imbalance or censoring handling taught about evaluation metrics; what the next refinement would be with two more weeks.

05

Risks worth naming

The claims quadrant leans harder on data availability than the underwriting one did — named plainly rather than assumed away.

Claims-lifecycle fields may not exist in the current dataset
Litigation flags, SIU outcomes, subrogation records, and multi-snapshot status history are the fields most public/pricing-oriented motor datasets omit. Audit schema in M0 first; M4 (litigation) and M6 (subrogation) are the two milestones most likely to need a supplementary or clearly-labelled synthetic dataset.
Censoring in duration/litigation models is easy to get wrong
Claims still open at data-extract time are censored observations, not "long-duration" ones — treating them as complete durations biases the model. Use proper survival-analysis censoring handling in M3/M4, and make this distinction part of the handwritten debrief.
Fraud and litigation targets are severely imbalanced
Confirmed fraud/SIU referrals and litigated claims are typically a small single-digit percentage of a book. Track PR-AUC and calibration, not accuracy; consider class-weighting or resampling and say explicitly which was chosen and why.
Same Free Edition serving/monitoring risk as the underwriting roadmap
Already tested and worked around in that roadmap's M1. Reuse the same batch-scoring fallback pattern here rather than re-litigating it.
Fraud/SIU model needs care around fairness and defensibility
A referral model that scores real people needs a defensible, explainable feature set — not just raw predictive power. Favour GLM/scorecard interpretability alongside the GBM challenger in M5, and treat explainability as an evaluation criterion, not an afterthought.
